## Abstract

We determine exact and approximate passive state costs for finite routing tables whose target lines lie in one complex two-plane. The minimum McMillan degree of a square rational-inner router equals the least algebraic degree of a rational projective map interpolating the ordered frequency-target data. The upper bound is constructive: scalar Fejer-Riesz factorization lifts any coprime rational pair to an explicit lossless network without degree overhead. The lower bound uses a common state-space denominator: more boundary zeros than states force the routed column to remain identically inside the target plane. We then prove a quantitative obstruction. If the normalized degree-d interpolation matrix has smallest singular value sigma, every lossless router of degree at most d has worst-node projective chordal error at least sigma divided by the square root of L(d+1)+sigma squared; the bound persists under calibrated perturbations. At degree zero we solve the full minimax problem: the exact error is the sine of half the radius of the smallest Bloch-sphere cap containing the targets, with a support certificate using at most three active targets. For two targets this exact frontier proves that the singular-value obstruction is asymptotically sharp as the targets collide. We also derive an exact binary collision law, a generic half-linear memory law, an unbounded gap over constant detectors, a complete four-node cross-ratio phase diagram, and the optimal integrated Wigner-Smith delay. Exact symbolic fixtures and reproducible certificates accompany the results.

## Abstract

We determine exact and approximate passive state costs for finite routing tables whose target lines lie in one complex two-plane. The minimum McMillan degree of a square rational-inner router equals the least algebraic degree of a rational projective map interpolating the ordered frequency-target data. The upper bound is constructive: scalar Fejer-Riesz factorization lifts any coprime rational pair to an explicit lossless network without degree overhead. The lower bound uses a common state-space denominator: more boundary zeros than states force the routed column to remain identically inside the target plane. We prove a quantitative obstruction: if the normalized degree-d interpolation matrix has smallest singular value sigma, every lossless router of degree at most d has worst-node chordal error at least sigma divided by the square root of L(d+1)+sigma squared, stably under calibrated perturbations. At degree zero the exact minimax error is the sine of half the radius of the smallest Bloch-sphere cap containing the targets. We then identify a sharp positive-degree closure transition. At d0=ceil((L-1)/2), every planar table has zero minimax infimum, while below d0 an open dense set has a positive error floor. Exceptional tables with exact degree above d0 are arbitrarily approximable by d0-state routers but have no minimizer there. In particular, every three-node table has E1=0, whereas a 2+1 collision needs two states exactly. Binary collision laws, an unbounded detector gap, the four-node cross-ratio phase diagram, optimal integrated delay, and exact symbolic certificates complete the results.

\begin{abstract}
Spectral gaps are often inferred from noisy Euclidean correlators by fitting a
small number of exponentials or solving a generalized eigenvalue problem.  Such
point estimates do not by themselves control the error incurred when the
spectral filter is selected from the same data.  We give a finite-sample test
that instead asks whether an entire simultaneous confidence set is compatible
with a proposed upper edge of the visible transfer spectrum.  For Gaussian
sketches of a block correlator, an extreme-singular-value inequality yields a
Loewner confidence band for the unknown joint Gram matrix.  Intersecting this
band with a truncated Hausdorff localizer is one semidefinite feasibility
problem.  We derive its semidefinite alternative: every rejection is accompanied
by a covariance-aware matrix witness, a negative separation margin, and
checkable primal-independent residuals.  The test has type-I error at most
$\alpha$ at fixed truncation order and requires neither known covariance nor a
Gaussian approximation to the quadratic moment features.  Simulations in the
interacting axial next-nearest-neighbour Ising chain compare the certificate to
covariance-known ellipsoids, plug-in eigenvectors, fixed Chebyshev filters, and
Ritz estimates.  We also document a negative result: Hotelling calibration can
severely undercover the non-Gaussian quadratic features at moderate sample
size.  The method certifies exclusion of overly large \emph{visible} gaps; it
does not turn non-rejection into a lower bound on a Hamiltonian mass gap.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $H$ be a Hamiltonian with ground energy $E_0$, choose a Euclidean step
$\tau>0$, and write $T=\exp[-\tau(H-E_0)]$.  Matrix-valued correlator moments
have the form
\begin{equation}
  B_k=\Psi^*T^k\Psi,\qquad k=0,1,\ldots,
  \label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}
where the columns of $\Psi$ are ground-state-subtracted probe states.  The
largest point in the support visible to these probes is
$\rho_{\rm vis}=e^{-\tau\Delta_{\rm vis}}$.  If a probe misses the first
excitation then $\Delta_{\rm vis}$ may exceed the Hamiltonian gap; this
visibility qualification is unavoidable.

Recent work has developed semidefinite bootstraps for exact Euclidean
correlators~\cite{Cho2025}, convergent lower-gap certificates for qubit
Hamiltonians~\cite{Rao2025}, and convergent certified upper bounds on the bulk
gap~\cite{Xu2026}.  Our question is complementary:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Given finitely many correlated random observations of a block
correlator, can one reject a proposed visible gap with simultaneous,
finite-sample error control while choosing the separating filter adaptively?}
\end{quote}
The distinction matters.  A negative eigenvalue of a noisy localizer is a
data-dependent observation, not yet a confidence statement.  Entrywise error
bars ignore correlation; a filter trained on the same sample invalidates a
pointwise interval; and a Gaussian approximation to quadratic observables may
be inaccurate precisely in the moderate-$n$ regime where certification is most
valuable.

Our contribution is a statistical-conic layer that can be placed in front of a
truncated moment localizer.  In the Gaussian-sketch model the primitive sketch
vector is Gaussian, but the reported block moments are quadratic and
Wishart-like.  We construct a confidence set for the primitive covariance
itself, then test its compatibility with the spectral null.  The resulting test
is nonasymptotic and covariance-free.  Its dual supplies an explicit rejection
witness rather than asking the reader to trust an ``infeasible'' solver label.

\paragraph{Scope.}
At finite moment degree, localizer positivity is a necessary condition for a
proposed upper spectral edge.  Its violation rigorously excludes that edge, but
non-violation does not prove a gap.  Consequently our output is a certified
upper bound on a visible gap (or a rejection of an overly large claimed gap),
not a proof of a positive mass gap.  This boundary also distinguishes the
present result from Hamiltonian-algebra hierarchies that can certify lower
bounds~\cite{Rao2025}.

\section{Block moments and the upper-edge localizer}

Assume for clarity that $H$ and the probes admit a real representation; the
complex proper-Gaussian case has different fourth-moment constants and is not
claimed here.  The spectral theorem gives a positive-semidefinite matrix-valued
measure $\mu$ on $[0,1]$ such that
\begin{equation}
  B_k=\int_{[0,1]}x^k\,d\mu(x).
\end{equation}
For a proposed edge $\theta\in(0,1]$ and degree $N$, define
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{N,\theta}(B)
  =\big[\theta B_{i+j}-B_{i+j+1}\big]_{i,j=0}^{N}.
  \label{eq:localizer}
\end{equation}
If $\operatorname{supp}\mu\subseteq[0,\theta]$, then for every vector
polynomial $p(x)=\sum_{i=0}^N x^iv_i$,
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{i,j}v_i^*(\theta B_{i+j}-B_{i+j+1})v_j
  =\int(\theta-x)\,p(x)^*d\mu(x)p(x)\ge0.
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathcal L_{N,\theta}(B)\succeq0$ is a necessary finite-window null.
A violation says that the probes see spectral weight above $\theta$, hence
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\rm vis}< -\tau^{-1}\log\theta.
\end{equation}

This elementary localizer is not presented as novel; truncated moment and
localization techniques have a long history~\cite{HeltonNie2011,deKlerk2018}.
The new issue below is how to test it after observing a correlated random Gram
matrix.

\section{Gaussian sketches and a covariance confidence band}

Put $u_{k,a}=T^{k/2}\psi_a$ for time index $k=0,\ldots,2N+1$ and channel
$a=1,\ldots,c$.  Draw independent standard real Gaussian vectors $g_r$ and
observe
\begin{equation}
  z_{r,k,a}=\ip{g_r}{u_{k,a}}.
  \label{eq:sketch}
\end{equation}
After stacking $(k,a)$ into one index, $z_r\sim\mathcal N(0,K)$ with
\begin{equation}
  K_{(k,a),(\ell,b)}
   =\ip{u_{k,a}}{u_{\ell,b}}
   = B_{(k+\ell)/2}[a,b].
  \label{eq:jointgram}
\end{equation}
In particular, the $k$th diagonal time block of $K$ is $B_k$.  Although
$z_{r,k}z_{r,k}^{\mathsf T}$ is an unbiased rank-one estimator of $B_k$, these
quadratic features are not jointly Gaussian.

Let
\begin{equation}
  S=\frac1n\sum_{r=1}^n z_rz_r^{\mathsf T},\qquad
  d=(2N+2)c.
\end{equation}
The following confidence set acts on all times and channels simultaneously.

\begin{theorem}[Wishart--Loewner confidence band]
\label{thm:wishart}
Let $z_1,\ldots,z_n$ be independent $\mathcal N(0,K)$ vectors in
$\mathbb R^d$, where $K\succeq0$ is unknown and the zero mean is known.  For
$0<\alpha<1$, set
\begin{equation}
 \eta=\frac{\sqrt d+\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)}}{\sqrt n}<1,
 \quad a=(1+\eta)^{-2},\quad b=(1-\eta)^{-2}.
 \label{eq:bandconstants}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb P\{aS\preceq K\preceq bS\}\ge1-\alpha.
  \label{eq:loewnerband}
\end{equation}
The statement remains valid for singular $K$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write the $n\times d$ data matrix as $Z=GK^{1/2}$, with $G$ standard
Gaussian.  The Gaussian extreme-singular-value inequality gives, with failure
probability at most $2e^{-t^2/2}$,
\[
 \sqrt n-\sqrt d-t\le s_{\min}(G)\le s_{\max}(G)
 \le\sqrt n+\sqrt d+t.
\]
With $t=\sqrt{2\log(2/\alpha)}$, division by $\sqrt n$, squaring, and
congruence by $K^{1/2}$ yield
$(1-\eta)^2K\preceq S\preceq(1+\eta)^2K$.  Rearrangement gives
\eqref{eq:loewnerband}.  Congruence does not require invertibility of $K$, so
the same argument covers the singular case.  We use the ambient $d$ rather
than an unknown effective rank.  See~\cite{RudelsonVershynin2010,Vershynin2010}
for the nonasymptotic singular-value bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Known mean is substantive]
If a sample mean is first estimated and subtracted, the degrees of freedom and
constants change.  The present theorem applies to the centered-by-construction
sketches in \eqref{eq:sketch}; it must not be copied unchanged to an unknown-mean
observation model.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact compatibility test and semidefinite alternative}

Let $P(K)=(K_{kk})_{k=0}^{2N+1}$ extract the diagonal time blocks.  Define the
random confidence set
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal C_\alpha(S)=\{Q\in\Smat^d:aS\preceq Q\preceq bS\}.
\end{equation}
We reject the proposed edge $\theta$ precisely when
\begin{equation}
 \text{there is no }Q\in\mathcal C_\alpha(S)
 \text{ with }\mathcal L_{N,\theta}(P(Q))\succeq0.
 \label{eq:primaltest}
\end{equation}
This is one SDP feasibility problem.

\begin{corollary}[Finite-sample validity]
\label{cor:level}
Under the null $\mathcal L_{N,\theta}(P(K))\succeq0$, the probability that
\eqref{eq:primaltest} rejects is at most $\alpha$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
On the event in \cref{thm:wishart}, the true $K$ is feasible in
\eqref{eq:primaltest}; rejection is therefore impossible.  The rejection event
is contained in the confidence-set failure event.
\end{proof}

No sample splitting is needed: arbitrary adaptive optimization inside the
confidence set preserves this argument.  This is the central statistical
advantage over selecting a polynomial direction from $S$ and attaching an
unadjusted pointwise error bar.

For numerical auditability, let $D$ be any fixed positive diagonal scaling
computed from $S$, put $Q=D\widetilde QD$, and denote the scaled interval by
$A\preceq\widetilde Q\preceq B$.  Let $\mathcal L_D$ be the resulting affine
linear map.

\begin{proposition}[Semidefinite alternative]
\label{prop:dual}
Assume $S\succ0$.  The feasibility problem \eqref{eq:primaltest} is infeasible
if and only if there exist $Y_A,Y_B,Y_L\succeq0$ such that
\begin{align}
 Y_A-Y_B+\mathcal L_D^*(Y_L)&=0,\label{eq:stationarity}\\
 -\ip{Y_A}{A}+\ip{Y_B}{B}&<0.\label{eq:dualmargin}
\end{align}
The multipliers may be normalized by
$\tr Y_A+\tr Y_B+\tr Y_L=1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Pair the three primal inequalities
$\widetilde Q-A\succeq0$, $B-\widetilde Q\succeq0$, and
$\mathcal L_D(\widetilde Q)\succeq0$ with the three PSD multipliers.
Equation~\eqref{eq:stationarity} cancels every term containing
$\widetilde Q$; primal feasibility would then force the constant in
\eqref{eq:dualmargin} to be nonnegative, proving one direction.  Conversely,
the interval is compact and has the strict midpoint $(A+B)/2$ when $S\succ0$.
Strong conic separation therefore supplies the stated multipliers whenever its
intersection with the inverse image of the PSD localizer cone is empty.
Homogeneity permits trace normalization.  If $S$ is singular, the same claim
requires first restricting all matrices to $\operatorname{ran}S$; we do not
silently replace this reduction by a numerical ridge.
\end{proof}

The implementation minimizes the dual margin under trace normalization.  A
reported rejection stores the margin, the Frobenius residual of
\eqref{eq:stationarity}, normalization error, and the smallest eigenvalue of
each numerical multiplier.  Interval or rational outward rounding remains the
last step from an auditable floating-point witness to a machine-checkable one.

\section{Why covariance awareness changes the witness}

The noiseless localizer test uses a minimum eigenvector.  Under simultaneous
uncertainty, that direction need not be optimal.  For comparison, if a moment
vector $y$ has known mean covariance $C/n$ and an ellipsoidal radius $q$, a
trace-one PSD witness $X$ has population signal-to-noise ratio
\begin{equation}
 \kappa(X)=\frac{[-\tr(\mathcal L(y)X)]_+}
 {\sqrt{\ell(X)^{\mathsf T}C\ell(X)}},
 \qquad \ell(X)_k=\tr(L_kX).
 \label{eq:snr}
\end{equation}
The robust upper bound becomes negative exactly when
$\sqrt n\,\kappa(X)>q$.  Optimizing $X$ therefore trades localizer negativity
against covariance sensitivity, rather than simply minimizing the observed
eigenvalue.

Mixed witnesses can be strictly better in principle.  For the synthetic family
$M=-\varepsilon I_r$ with isotropic Frobenius uncertainty, every rank-one
projector has uncertainty norm one, whereas $X=I_r/r$ has norm $r^{-1/2}$.
The sample threshold differs by a factor $r$.  In our ANNNI pilots, however,
many optimized ellipsoidal witnesses above threshold are numerically rank one.
The observed advantage there is primarily \emph{covariance-aware direction
selection} relative to the plug-in minimum eigenvector, not evidence that a
high-rank mixture is always required.

\section{Interacting ANNNI experiment}

We use the periodic axial next-nearest-neighbour Ising chain
\begin{equation}
 H=-J_1\sum_i Z_iZ_{i+1}-J_2\sum_i Z_iZ_{i+2}-h_x\sum_iX_i
 \label{eq:annni}
\end{equation}
with $J_1=1$, $J_2=0.37$, $h_x=2.2$, and $\tau=0.2$.  This frustrated,
nonintegrable finite chain is not being used to claim a new phase boundary; it
is a controlled interacting test bed where exact sparse diagonalization and
Krylov propagation provide ground truth.  Ground-state-subtracted central-site
$X$ and $Z$ probes form a two-channel correlator.  The global spin-flip parity
separates their visible sectors.

At degree $N=2$, the primitive joint sketch dimension is $d=12$, the flattened
quadratic moment dimension is 18, and the localizer has size $6\times6$.
Lengths $L=6,8,10,12,14,16$ are tested at the exact visible edge and at gaps
inflated by 15\% and 35\%.  For each cell we generate independent sketch
ensembles at $n=500,1000,2000,5000$.  The production seed, full raw summaries,
and SHA-256 hashes are stored in the repository.

\subsection{Baselines and controls}

We compare four logically different objects.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item The Wishart--Loewner dual certificate of Proposition~\ref{prop:dual}, with unknown
 covariance and finite-sample level control.
 \item A covariance-known multivariate-Chebyshev ellipsoid, useful as a
 controlled oracle reference but not a deployable unknown-covariance method.
 \item A plug-in minimum-eigenvector and a fixed Chebyshev polynomial filter.
 These expose the value of covariance-aware direction selection.
 \item A generalized Ritz/GEVP edge estimate.  It is a point estimator, not a
 confidence certificate, and is reported separately rather than granted the
 same logical status.
\end{enumerate}

The known-covariance pilot showed a sharp sample transition: for the 35\%
inflated gap, the covariance-aware robust test reached essentially full power
near $n=1000$ across $L\le14$, while the plug-in eigenvector was usually still
silent until $n=5000$.  At the true edge the robust methods made no rejection
in the medium grid.  These observations motivated the covariance-unknown
production campaign; final rates and binomial intervals will replace this
paragraph after the full artifact is frozen.

\subsection{Negative calibration result}

The quadratic sketch features obey Isserlis' covariance formula but are
Wishart-like rather than multivariate Gaussian.  At $L=12$, $N=2$, and only
$n=120$ sketches, a nominal 95\% Hotelling ellipsoid covered the exact moment
vector in 83.4\% of 500 simulated ensembles.  Its apparent 97.4\% detection
rate for an inflated gap was therefore invalid as a 95\% certificate.  Coverage
approached nominal only slowly with $n$.  This failed route is retained in the
artifact as a warning against importing an exact Gaussian-mean theorem into a
quadratic-feature problem.

\section{Numerical verification protocol}

Ill-conditioned Euclidean moment matrices make raw primal status unreliable.
In a first primal--dual Wishart replay, two of five inflated-gap samples were
labeled \texttt{optimal\_inaccurate} by a primal solver even though their dual
margins were below $-4\times10^{-4}$.  In a separate ten-ensemble $L=8$ smoke
at $n=500$, null dual objectives lay between $1.8\times10^{-10}$ and
$6.5\times10^{-10}$, while 35\%-inflated-gap objectives lay between
$-8.2\times10^{-4}$ and $-4.1\times10^{-4}$.  The maximum stationarity residual
was $2.3\times10^{-9}$.  The first production pass conservatively thresholded
the raw dual margin at $-10^{-7}$ and stored all residual diagnostics.  The
final pass uses the feasibility repair described next.

\paragraph{Feasibility repair.}
Let floating-point multipliers first be projected onto the PSD cone, and let
$R=Y_A-Y_B+\mathcal L_D^*(Y_L)=R_+-R_-$ be their remaining stationarity
residual.  The replacements
\begin{equation}
  \widehat Y_A=Y_A+R_-,\qquad
  \widehat Y_B=Y_B+R_+
  \label{eq:repair}
\end{equation}
remain PSD and satisfy stationarity exactly, up to the arithmetic used for the
eigendecomposition.  Trace renormalization preserves this equality.  In a
20-ensemble repaired smoke, the maximum stationarity residual fell below
$8\times10^{-17}$ and normalization error below $4\times10^{-16}$; null
objectives were positive, whereas all 15\% and 35\% inflated-gap objectives
were negative.  The production threshold is $-10^{-9}$ on the \emph{repaired}
objective, leaving an additional buffer above double-precision roundoff.

This gap between scientific margin and numerical residual is encouraging but
does not itself constitute interval arithmetic.  The release protocol is:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item solve in floating point after diagonal congruence scaling;
 \item independently rebuild the adjoint and residual from exported matrices;
 \item project only for diagnostics, never to manufacture a better objective;
 \item outward-round selected repaired witnesses and verify the resulting inequalities
 with interval or rational arithmetic;
 \item rerun a subset with a second conic solver.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and interpretation}

\paragraph{Visible versus Hamiltonian gap.}
The probes may have zero overlap with the first excitation.  Multiple symmetry
channels reduce but do not remove this risk.  Every result is consequently
labeled $\Delta_{\rm vis}$ unless a separate overlap theorem is available.

\paragraph{Upper versus lower bounds.}
Rejecting support in $[0,\theta]$ proves that a claimed gap is too large.  A
finite localizer's non-rejection is not a proof that the true support lies below
$\theta$.  Our method therefore complements, rather than replaces, lower-gap
Hamiltonian certificates~\cite{Rao2025}.

\paragraph{Acquisition model.}
The exact confidence band uses independent, known-zero-mean Gaussian sketch
coordinates.  Classical stochastic estimators or randomized numerical linear
algebra can realize this model directly; a laboratory implementation must state
how those coordinates are obtained and account for state-preparation and
measurement noise.  Markov-chain autocorrelation, non-Gaussian primitive
sketches, and unknown centering require different concentration results.

\paragraph{Finite volume.}
The ANNNI study validates statistical behavior at finite $L$.  It neither
establishes a thermodynamic gap nor competes with the convergent bulk hierarchy
of Xu et al.~\cite{Xu2026}.  A future combination of noisy-data confidence sets
with thermodynamic operator-algebra hierarchies is an open direction.

\section{Conclusion}

The main result is a finite-sample bridge from random correlator sketches to an
auditable spectral-edge exclusion.  A single Loewner band absorbs all
time-channel correlations and all adaptive witness selection; a semidefinite
alternative turns rejection into a replayable matrix inequality.  The approach
is intentionally one-sided.  It can falsify an overly large visible gap with
controlled type-I error, but it does not infer a mass gap from silence.  The
interacting-chain campaign is designed to quantify when that rigorous statement
is statistically useful and when conventional point estimates are merely more
optimistic.
